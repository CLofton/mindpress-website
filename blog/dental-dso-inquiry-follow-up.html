Dental and DSO inquiry follow-up that reaches Monday ready

New-patient inquiries and hygiene recall, called in order. Send last week’s inquiries. Friday you get Monday’s call list.

Dental groups and DSOs do not lack demand. They lack hours after the chair schedule wins the day. New-patient forms pile up. Hygiene recall sits in a queue. Same-day openings go to whoever happens to answer first.

Follow-up is the job that slips. MindPress exists to put that job back on the calendar. You send last week’s inquiries. Friday you get Monday’s call list. Your people take the live conversation.

Where dental inquiries stall

A parent submits a new-patient form during school pickup. A recall text gets a “call me” reply while the front desk seats a walk-in. A referral arrives from a physician and waits in a shared inbox.

Each item is small. Together they become last week’s unfinished work. By the time someone sorts the list, the patient already booked elsewhere—or went quiet.

Your coordinators know how to talk. They need an ordered list, not another place to look.

What Monday’s list should include

New-patient inquiries first when timing matters. Hygiene recall next when openings exist. Clear notes on when the person asked. Enough context for your caller to sound like your office, not a stranger.

We do not need a patient chart dump to start. Counts and timestamps get the first pass moving. You keep clinical detail inside your systems. We focus on who to call and in what order.

Why this fits multi-location groups

DSOs stretch the same problem across sites. One location’s backlog looks small. Ten locations make a week of missed dials. Central teams burn time collecting exports. Local teams burn time guessing priority.

A Friday list gives both sides a shared object. Ops can see volume. Front desk can dial. Nobody waits on a perfect data warehouse before the next Monday.

Your people still own the chair conversation

Scheduling judgment stays with your team. Insurance questions stay with your team. The tone of your brand stays with your team. We put AI to work on the sort and the handoff so they spend Monday talking to people who already asked for care.

That matches how strong dental ops already think: prep the board, then run the board.

A simple weekly rhythm

End of week: gather inquiry counts and timestamps from forms, phones, and portals you already use. Send them. Friday: receive Monday’s call list. Monday: your people dial in order.

Repeat. The rhythm matters more than a one-time cleanup. Unworked inquiries return every week. So should the list.

Start with last week

You do not need a full year of history. Last week is enough to prove the path. Send it. Get the list. Let your coordinators work it. Then decide how the next weeks look.

Hygiene recall without the guilt spiral

Recall lists feel endless because they are. The fix is not shame. The fix is an ordered slice for Monday that matches open capacity. Call the people you can seat. Leave the rest for the next cycle instead of pretending the whole database moves in one morning.

When recall and new-patient asks share one ordered list, your front desk stops choosing at random under pressure.

What to send from a dental office

Form counts. Missed-call counts if you track them. Referral counts. Timestamps. Channel labels when easy. Skip clinical detail in the first send. Keep PHI inside your practice systems until you decide otherwise.
